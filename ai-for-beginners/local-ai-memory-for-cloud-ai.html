How to give your cloud AI a long-term memory with a local AI on your PC

Why cloud AI keeps repeating mistakes you already corrected, and how a local AI on your own PC can be its long-term memory. With my real build as the example.

Last checked October 2026. Written in plain words. No question is silly.

The short answer: Cloud AI assistants forget. They see only a small slice of your history at the start of each chat, so they repeat mistakes you already corrected, then apologize. A local memory partner is an AI you run on your own PC. It keeps your full project history privately and hands your cloud AI a short, sourced brief before each job. It will not stop every mistake, but it should stop many of the repeat ones.

The problem: your cloud AI only sees a small slice

Cloud AI assistants often load only a little memory when a new chat starts, such as the last few days plus a handful of key facts. Older notes may be saved somewhere, but the assistant only looks them up when it thinks to. When it does not, a lesson you taught it weeks ago never comes up.

A real example from my own setup. My cloud assistants (Grok Bot) hold about 700 saved notes, but each chat starts with only the latest few days plus key facts. On October 31, 2026, I had to correct them on all of these in one day:

What the bot didWhat I had already decided
Suggested YouTube for a news video on my news siteHost the video file myself on GitHub Pages with a plain HTML5 player
Put my news site's signoff line in my lawn business postsThat signoff belongs to the news site only
Left the service towns out of the lawn posts and wrote "even in the rain"Name the towns, skip the rain wording
Gave time estimates in human "days"No human day estimates
Said "that's exactly why" after being correctedJust fix it, do not spin it

Every one led to an apology and a redo. The bot was not lazy. It simply did not have those old decisions in front of it.

What is a local memory partner?

It is an AI assistant you run on your own PC. You name it whatever you like, and you can build your own. It keeps your full project history privately on your computer and hands your cloud AI a short brief before each job. The cloud AI still does the heavy lifting online. The local partner is its long-term memory.

Mine is named Kellie, and this guide uses her as the example. Kellie runs on a free local AI program called Ollama with a desktop app. She drafts posts, makes images, handles reminders, reads files and does voice work, and her memory stays on my computer.

How it works, step by step

  1. Your cloud AI gets a job. For example, "write this week's lawn posts."
  2. It asks the local partner for a one-page brief about that project before it starts.
  3. The local partner answers with sourced rules and history. It quotes what it stored, with dates and where it came from. If something is missing, it says "not in memory" instead of guessing.
  4. The cloud AI does the job.
  5. It sends a short job report back. The local partner files it by project: decisions, rules, failures, files and next steps, each with a date and a source.

One important rule: only your own words become confirmed rules. The bots can report what happened, but they cannot write rules for you.

Honest limits

  • It reduces repeat mistakes. It will not end every error or every apology. A brief only helps if the right rule was stored and the cloud AI actually follows it.
  • It is only as good as what goes in. If you never stated a rule, it cannot be in memory.
  • This is a build-it-yourself project today. Mine is still being built. There is no one-click app for this yet.
  • Private stays private only on your side. The memory lives on your PC, but whatever goes into a brief is sent to your cloud AI for that job. Keep passwords and account numbers out of it.
  • Your PC has to be on for the cloud AI to ask it for a brief.

Want to try this yourself?

  1. Start with a local AI. Our guide What is Ollama? explains it in plain words, and this setup guide walks through installing it on Windows.
  2. Keep one simple notes file per project with your rules and decisions, in your own words, with a date on each.
  3. Before each job, paste the short version into your cloud AI, or have your local AI write that short version for you.
  4. After each job, add what changed. That habit is the heart of it, whatever tools you use.
