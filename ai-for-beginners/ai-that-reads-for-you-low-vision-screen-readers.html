AI that reads for you: new help for low vision and screen-reader users (2026)

Free tools that read your screen out loud or make it bigger, plus three new AI helpers from November 2026, explained in plain words with honest limits.

Last checked November 17, 2026. Written in plain words. No question is silly.

The short answer: You may not need to buy anything. Windows, iPhone and Android already have free tools that read the screen out loud and make it bigger: Narrator and Magnifier on Windows, VoiceOver, Speak Screen and Zoom on iPhone, and TalkBack and Select to Speak on Android. Three newer tools arrived in early November 2026. JAWS, ZoomText and Fusion 2027 add an AI helper called Vispero Companion, but it is an early test version that needs a Vispero account and works over the internet. Eyedaptic Eva is an AI voice assistant inside Eyedaptic's EYE7 vision glasses. AskSurFa is a web browser that reads pages aloud and makes text bigger when you say "Surfa". Try the free built-in tools first.

Who this is for

  • People whose eyes are getting worse and who find small print on a screen tiring.
  • People with macular degeneration or another low-vision condition.
  • Blind and low-vision people who already use a screen reader like JAWS and want to know what changed this fall.
  • Family members setting up a phone or computer for a parent.

If a screen is hard to read, also talk to your eye doctor or a low-vision specialist. These tools help, but they do not replace a checkup.

New this fall: JAWS, ZoomText and Fusion 2027 with Vispero Companion

JAWS is a well known paid screen reader for Windows. ZoomText magnifies the screen. Fusion combines the two. Vispero, the company behind them, released the 2027 versions on November 3, 2026.

What is new. Vispero Companion puts the AI help in one place. You can ask it how a feature works, ask for a photo to be described, get help with a busy web page, change a setting, or ask it to do a task. It keeps the conversation going, so you can ask follow-up questions without starting over. ZoomText also gains Photo Tracking, which finds people, animals, food and other things in a picture and moves the magnified view from one to the next. Vispero says that part runs on your own computer and works offline. ZoomText can now also magnify apps that use screen-capture protection, and ZoomText and Fusion have a new "Best performance" setting if they feel slow.

What it costs. Vispero's release post does not list a price for Companion. Check current license options with Vispero before you upgrade.

What to check before you count on Companion:

  • It is part of Vispero's Early Adopter Program, which means it is still being built. Expect rough edges.
  • You need a Vispero account and must turn it on in JAWS Options, Early Adopter Program.
  • Requests are handled over the internet, so some of what is on your screen leaves your computer when you use it. Think twice before using it on bank or medical pages.
  • Reports say it starts in the United States first, with a full release targeted for January 2027. That date comes from secondary summaries of a Vispero presentation, not an official notice.

Sources: Vispero's release post (Nov 3, 2026) and a summary by The Blindbandit Chronicle (Nov 6, 2026).

New this fall: Eyedaptic Eva on EYE7 glasses

Eyedaptic makes EYE7, smart glasses that enlarge and enhance what you look at for people with macular degeneration and other retinal conditions. On November 2, 2026 it added Eva, an AI voice assistant built into the glasses.

What it does. You talk to Eva in normal words. Eyedaptic's example: say "I can't read that" and Eva changes the view to help, without you knowing the exact command. Eyedaptic says it helps with reading, shopping, spotting objects, watching TV and getting around.

What the company claims. These numbers come from Eyedaptic, not from an independent test. Eyedaptic says that in its own testing Eva was more than 99% accurate at reading and 97% accurate with commands, and that it supports up to 99 languages. It also says an independent clinical study found an average of 5.7 lines of improvement in close-up vision for patients with advanced retinal disease using Eyedaptic. Eyedaptic says Eva is available now as a remote upgrade for all EYE7 users.

What it costs. The launch post does not list a price. Ask Eyedaptic or your low-vision clinic.

What to check: Ask for a hands-on try before you buy, ideally at a low-vision clinic. Ask about the price, the return period, whether Eva needs the internet, and what happens to the pictures the glasses see.

Source: Eyedaptic's launch announcement (Nov 2, 2026).

New this fall: AskSurFa reads web pages out loud

AskSurFa is a web browser from SEW Capital, a UK company. It was announced on November 2, 2026.

What it does. Say "Surfa" and then a command, or tap a button. "Read aloud" reads the page. "Read the headlines" reads just the headlines. "Stop reading" stops. "Bigger text" and "smaller text" change the size, and "dark mode" makes the screen darker. The company says these work on any website, so you do not have to hunt for settings on every site. It can also deal with cookie pop-ups for you.

What it costs. On asksurfa.com the built-in voice commands are free. An optional AI agent that chains steps together is listed at £9.99 a month (checked November 17, 2026). You do not need it for reading aloud.

What to check: It is new and from a small company. Try the free commands first. Do not sign up for the paid agent unless you know you need it. For banking and medical sites, your regular browser may be the safer choice.

Source: SEW Capital's press release (Nov 2, 2026).

Which one should I try first?

  1. Just want things read out loud sometimes? Speak Screen on iPhone, Select to Speak on Android, or Magnifier's read aloud on Windows. All free.
  2. Need to use the computer without seeing the screen? Start with Narrator, VoiceOver or TalkBack. They are free. JAWS is a paid option.
  3. Already use JAWS or ZoomText? The 2027 update is worth installing. Try Companion only if you are fine with an early test version that uses the internet.
  4. Have macular degeneration and want help seeing the world, not just a screen? Ask a low-vision clinic about wearable aids like EYE7.

Common questions

What is the easiest free way to have my computer read to me?

On Windows 10 or 11, Narrator is built in. Press Windows key + Ctrl + Enter to start it. If you only need text made bigger, Magnifier is also built in, and it can read text aloud too.

Can my phone read a page out loud?

Yes. On iPhone, turn on Speak Screen in Settings, Accessibility, Read & Speak, then swipe down with two fingers from the top of the screen. On Android, turn on Select to Speak in Settings, Accessibility, then use its shortcut and tap the text.

Is Vispero Companion ready for everyone?

Not yet. It is part of Vispero's Early Adopter Program, so it is still being built. It needs a Vispero account and works over the internet. Reports say a full US release is targeted for January 2027, but that date comes from secondary summaries.

Are Eyedaptic's accuracy numbers from an independent test?

No. The 99% reading accuracy and 97% command accuracy figures come from Eyedaptic's own testing. Ask for a hands-on try at a low-vision clinic before you buy.

Is AskSurFa free?

Its built-in voice commands, like read aloud, bigger text and dark mode, are free on asksurfa.com. An optional AI agent is listed at 9.99 pounds a month as of November 17, 2026. You do not need the paid agent to have pages read aloud.

Do I need to buy anything?

No. Windows, iPhone and Android all have free screen readers and magnifiers built in. Try those first, then decide if you need more.
