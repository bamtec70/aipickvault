AI that may help with vision, hearing, hands and memory

Free, practical tools for reading mail, following conversations, typing by voice and remembering things. With honest limits.

Last checked October 2026. Written in plain words. No question is silly.

The short version: Some of the most useful AI is already built into your phone and computer, for free. It may help you read small print, follow conversations, type without a keyboard, and remember things. It is not perfect, and it does not replace advice from your doctor or an occupational therapist, but it is worth trying.

Each section below lists free tools first. Features change, so names may look a little different on your device.

Low vision: reading and seeing

  • Seeing AI (free from Microsoft, iPhone and Android). Point your camera at mail, a menu or a label and it reads it out loud. It can also describe a photo or a scene.
  • Be My Eyes (free). Its "Be My AI" feature describes photos and documents. It can also connect you to a volunteer by video for a quick hand.
  • Built into your phone: Magnifier turns the camera into a magnifying glass. Spoken content reads the screen aloud (Speak Screen on iPhone, Select to Speak on Android).
  • Built into Windows: Magnifier (Windows key + plus sign) and Narrator (Windows key + Ctrl + Enter), which reads the screen. On Windows 11, Narrator can describe images with Copilot when you ask it to.
  • Private option: on your own PC, Ollama with a vision model like gemma3:4b can read a photo of a letter without uploading it anywhere. See What is Ollama?

Hearing loss: live captions

  • Windows 11 Live Captions puts words on screen for any sound playing on your PC, like video calls and YouTube. Turn it on with Windows key + Ctrl + L. Microsoft says the audio is processed on your device and not sent to the cloud. It can also caption your microphone for in-person chats.
  • Phones: Live Caption on Android and Live Captions on iPhone caption videos, calls and more. Android's Live Transcribe captions a conversation in the room.
  • Captions can mishear names and accents. Use them as help, not as a record you rely on for important details.

Arthritis, tremors and limited mobility

  • Windows Voice Access lets you open apps, click, scroll and type by talking. It works offline on Windows 11.
  • Dictation: press Windows key + H on any PC to type by speaking. On phones, tap the microphone on the keyboard.
  • Voice Control (iPhone) and Voice Access (Android) run the whole phone by voice.
  • Ask your occupational therapist which setup suits your hands. They may also suggest a large-key keyboard or a stand that holds a tablet at the right angle.

Memory: reminders and notes

  • Ask your phone's assistant: "Remind me to take my pills at 8 AM every day."
  • Use an AI chat to turn a messy list into a clear checklist: "Make this into a simple daily list."
  • Ask AI to summarize a doctor visit from your own notes, then check it with the office.
  • Keep a simple notes app with a big font. Many let you add notes by voice.
  • Reminders support your memory. They do not replace medication systems your pharmacist or doctor recommends.
  • Live alone, or worried about someone who does? See AI helpers for seniors who live alone: talking tablets, daily check-in calls and camera-free sensors.

Dyslexia and reading or writing difficulty

  • Immersive Reader (in Microsoft Edge, Word and OneNote) reads text aloud, highlights each word and can space out lines.
  • Read aloud in Edge reads any web page to you.
  • Ask an AI chat: "Rewrite this letter in short, simple sentences" or "Fix my spelling but keep my words."

Honest limits

  • AI may misread small print, numbers or handwriting. Check anything important, like doses, amounts due and dates.
  • Nothing here is medical advice. These tools may help day to day. Ask your doctor or an occupational therapist what fits your needs.
  • Online AI features send what they see or hear to a company. For private paperwork, use the on-device options above or local AI.

Stay safe: scams and fake voices

  • Scammers can now copy a voice from a short clip. If a "family member" calls in trouble and needs money now, hang up and call them back on their usual number.
  • Agree on a family "safe word" that only you know.
  • No real bank, government office or tech company will ask for your password, PIN or full card number by phone, text or chat.
  • Never give bank information to an AI, a caller or a pop-up. If unsure, call the number on the back of your card.

Common questions

What free AI tools help people with low vision?

Seeing AI and Be My Eyes are free apps that read text and describe photos. Phones and Windows also have built-in Magnifier and screen readers like Narrator.

Does Windows have free live captions?

Yes. Windows 11 Live Captions shows captions for audio playing on your PC and can caption your microphone. Turn it on with Windows key + Ctrl + L. Microsoft says the audio is processed on the device.

Can I control my computer by voice?

Yes. Windows 11 Voice Access lets you open apps, click and type by speaking. Windows key + H starts dictation in any text box. iPhone and Android have similar voice control.

Is AI a replacement for medical or therapy advice?

No. These tools may help with daily tasks, but ask your doctor or an occupational therapist what fits your situation.
