What is AI, really?

A calm, plain-words explanation for anyone who feels unsure about AI. No jargon, no hype.

Last checked October 2026. Written in plain words. No question is silly.

The short version: AI is a computer program that has read a huge amount of text and learned to guess the next word. It is very good at guessing. It does not think, feel or want anything. It is a tool, like a very clever autocomplete.

What AI really is

You know how your phone suggests the next word when you type a text? Modern AI chat programs work on the same idea, just much bigger.

They were trained on books, websites and articles. From all that reading, they learned patterns. When you ask a question, they predict a likely answer one word at a time.

That is why the answers sound so natural. It is also why AI can be confidently wrong. It is guessing what sounds right, not checking what is true.

It is not a mind. It has no feelings, no wishes and no plans. When you close the window, it is not thinking about you.

What AI can do well

  • Explain a confusing letter, bill or form in plain words.
  • Help you write an email, a note or a thank you card.
  • Summarize a long article so you get the main points.
  • Read text out loud, or describe a photo, with the right app.
  • Answer "how do I" questions, like how to change a setting on your phone.
  • Brainstorm: gift ideas, recipes from what is in your fridge, trip plans.

What AI cannot do (or should not be trusted to do)

  • It cannot be sure it is right. Always double check facts, dates, phone numbers and anything about money or health.
  • It is not your doctor, lawyer or banker. Use it to prepare questions, then ask a real person.
  • It does not know you unless you tell it. It does not read your mind or watch you.
  • Most AI cannot act on its own. It answers when you ask. It does not wander off and do things.

Myths vs facts

What people worry aboutThe honest answer
"AI is spying on me."Online AI services do keep what you type, at least for a while, like most websites. That is a real privacy question, not spying in the movie sense. You can turn some of it off, or use AI that runs only on your own computer. Full privacy guide.
"AI is going to take over."Today's AI predicts words. It has no wants and no goals of its own. Experts do debate long term risks, and that debate is worth following, but the chat program on your computer is not plotting anything.
"AI will take everyone's job."Some tasks are changing, especially paperwork and writing. Most jobs are changing rather than vanishing. Learning the basics, like you are doing now, is the best protection.
"AI knows everything."No. It can make things up and sound sure about it. People call this "hallucinating." Check anything important.
"I am too old or not techy enough."If you can type a text message or talk to your phone, you can use AI. You just ask in normal words.

How to use AI safely

  1. Never type passwords, bank numbers, your Social Security number or card numbers into an AI chat.
  2. Check important answers with a second source or a real person.
  3. Watch for scams. Criminals now use AI to fake voices and write convincing emails. If a "grandchild" or "bank" calls asking for money in a hurry, hang up and call back on a number you already know.
  4. Start small. Ask it to explain something you already understand, so you can see how it does.
  5. Want full privacy? Use AI that runs on your own computer. Nothing you type leaves the machine. See What is Ollama?

Try it: your first question

Open any AI chat (or install one on your own PC) and type something like:

"Explain what a 401k is in simple words, like you are talking to a friend."

Then ask a follow up: "Can you make that shorter?" That back and forth is the whole trick.

Common questions

Is AI alive or conscious?

No. Today's AI is a program that predicts likely words based on patterns it learned from text. It has no feelings, wishes or awareness.

Can AI make mistakes?

Yes, often. It can state wrong facts in a confident voice. Always double check anything about health, money, legal matters, dates or phone numbers.

Is it safe for older adults to use AI?

Yes, with the same common sense you use online. Never share passwords or bank details, check important answers, and be alert to scam calls and emails that use fake voices.

Do I need to pay for AI?

No. There are free versions of online AI chat, and free programs like Ollama that run AI on your own computer with no monthly fee.
